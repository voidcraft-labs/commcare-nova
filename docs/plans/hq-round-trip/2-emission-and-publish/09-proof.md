## Where the lane runs

The lane runs in four places, and each holds every check it runs exactly as the others do (`proof/README.md`, "The lane in CI"):

- **The pull request lane, in CI** (`ci.yml`). Every pull request of the stack runs it. It runs without the served readers (`PROOF_READERS=0`): HQ, Core and HQ's editors over every group the change invalidates, which is every document, control and package group the evidence store does not hold for the pull request's inputs, with every check held to the registers and no state handed to Formplayer, the Web Apps client, a device or Connect. It has no time cap and drops nothing for time: a group runs, or the store holds it. Its expected length is modelled from `proof/timings.json` over real queues (`proof/README.md`, "Timings and what the lane is expected to take"): about 6 minutes when nothing is fresh (the run's audit sample of about four documents always runs), under 10 minutes up to about 100 changed documents, 10 minutes 10 seconds when a judge's file or a register changes, since every document is then judged again from its stored records, and 12 minutes 51 seconds when every group runs fresh, on fourteen shards. The last row is measured once: the lane's own pull request (#738) ran every group fresh on GitHub's runners, and its gate was done 12 minutes 40 seconds after the pull request opened. The other rows are the model's alone. Each of step 2's pull requests changes the register and an emitter, so the implementer expects each one's lane to take between 10 and 13 minutes. The gate leaves an entry whose artifact only a served reader writes (`formplayer@`, `webapps@`, `android@`, `android-tablet@`, `connect@`, `release@`) to the nightly, live and fixed alike, and with them every held walk stop, since each names a served reader.
- **The whole lane, on a proof machine.** Every check with every reader (Core, Formplayer, the Web Apps client, Android on a phone and a tablet, and Connect) over every document and control, observed fresh: `proof/remote/run.sh <host> -- --workers <n>`, then `python3 -m proof.lane.gate .proof/remote/<slot>-<stamp>` (`proof/README.md`, "Running the lane on another machine"). A proof machine is Linux on amd64 with Docker and the Android reader's runtime, the only platform where every reader runs, with one vCPU a worker and about 5 GB of memory a worker with every reader, 3 without; `proof/remote/run.sh <host> --provision --android-licenses <dir>` makes one of a fresh Ubuntu 24.04 machine. The whole lane with every reader costs 552 worker-hours at about a vCPU a worker, against 4.5 without the readers. It was measured on 2026-10-13 with every group observed afresh (357 documents, 84 controls and 30 package groups): 49% of the workers' time is inside a device's request, about 43% is the Web Apps client's walks, and about 8% is Formplayer with HQ's, Core's and the editors' own work. The median document takes 3,361 seconds on one worker and the longest 13,321. That run held the lane on two proof machines, 184 workers on 224 vCPUs, as sixteen bins of one queue, each two and three quarter to four and three quarter hours, gated together (`proof/README.md`, "Timings and what the lane is expected to take"). A slice for iteration is `proof/remote/run.sh <host> -- --workers <n> proof/checks -k "<id> or <id>"`, every reader by default and fewer with `--env PROOF_READERS=<readers>`. A check that needs no served reader also runs on any machine with Docker: `PROOF_READERS=0 npm run proof -- proof/checks -k <id>`.
- **The nightly** (`proof-audit.yml`), on five nights a week, Tuesday to Saturday at 03:23 UTC: both profiles over `main` against `main`'s store, without the readers and then with every reader, so every group the store lacks is observed. It takes what changed: minutes on most nights, and about thirteen hours where every key moved, as after an emission change or new pins (13.5 hours as planned from the measured seconds, 11.5 once a run has measured the pieces). A failure opens an issue that names each failing group, why it failed, and the command that reruns that group alone at the commit the run checked (`python3 -m proof.lane.report`).
- **The weekly** (`proof-audit.yml`), from Saturday at 19:07 UTC: both profiles whole and fresh, which proves the store's keys and replaces its snapshot, then its sampled comparisons, about twenty-two hours in all, to Sunday evening. A failure opens the same kind of issue.

No lane with the readers runs whole every night, and no two scheduled lanes run their shards at once (`proof/README.md`, "When the scheduled lanes run" and "Documents observed in pieces"). The 552 worker-hours are 138 hours of GitHub's four-vCPU runners. A whole lane with every reader plans as 56 shards of about three hours each, fourteen at once, about twelve hours, and a heavy document is observed in pieces, one configuration a worker, and judged whole after them. Fourteen of the account's twenty runners is one lane's bound, which leaves six to pull requests only while one scheduled lane runs, so the schedules are set apart: the nights and the week above, the weekly pin pull request on Monday at 00:37 UTC and the image comparison on Monday at 17:17 UTC. No night runs on Sunday or Monday. The week's lanes hold `main` as of Saturday evening, so the readers' verdict on a change that merges on a Sunday or a Monday arrives with Tuesday's night.

## The register and the findings this section owns

### The fixed-defect register and finding 52's orphaned control

**Today.** A check runs on a control only while a known-defect entry of that check names it (`proof/checks/cases.py::control_params`), and a `control:<id>` group is queued only while an entry names it (`proof/store/queue.py::control_groups`, which in a run without the readers counts only the entries that run holds, `registers.held_entries`). Removing a defect's entries therefore stops its control everywhere, silently: #712 removed finding 52's two entries, and `proof/controls/expander-form-hashtag-expansion-declares-the-casedb-02e7ce76-0` has run nowhere since. Nothing in the lane notices a control directory no entry names.

**Fix.** A second register, `proof/fixed-defects.json`: a JSON list, `[]` when empty, required to exist.

An entry is a known-defect entry without `document`, carrying the id it had:

```json
{"id": "d1-ids-app-xmlns", "defect": 1, "part": "ids and xmlns",
 "check": "proof1", "artifact": "app.json", "path": "/modules/*/forms/*/xmlns",
 "control": "case-operation-query"}
```

with `kind` and `values` where the live entry had them. There is no `fixed` field: a stack's pull request number is unknown when the entry is written, and git holds it.

Rules, enforced by the loader and the gate:

1. The same field checks as a live entry: `check` in `registers.py::HELD_CHECKS`, `path` from the root, `kind` one of `proof/checks/differences.py::KINDS`, no undecided manifest use, and the control directory exists.
2. An id is in one register only. No two fixed entries share check, artifact, path, kind, values and control.
3. A fixed entry is held on its control alone, by every run that runs its control: the check ran on the control and some difference there matches the entry (its class, and `values` where pinned, compared without a document). A sampled run and a whole run run every control. A run over documents named by hand holds the fixed entries of the controls it ran and leaves the rest. A queue built for a selection (`proof.store.queue --documents only:`) queues the controls of the live entries naming its documents, so it holds a fixed entry only where one of those controls is also the fixed entry's. A fixed entry is held one way: it fails by name, under `control:<id>`, where its control ran and does not show it, and where its control ran without its check. Removing a fixed entry fails nothing by itself, since a control is held to the entries that name it and its other differences are ignored; a removal is one of the repair rule's two repairs, and rule 5 fails where it leaves a control directory no entry names. A run that observes some readers or none leaves a fixed entry whose artifact only an unobserved reader writes to the nightly, as `registers.held_entries` leaves a live one: 234 of the 720 fixed entries at the exit are such entries.
4. A fixed entry is never an owner on a corpus document. `reconcile` already fails any corpus difference no live entry holds, so a regression into a fixed class fails as unregistered. The fixed register is read on corpus documents for one thing: the failure message. Where an unregistered difference falls in a fixed entry's class, `registers.py::Reconciliation.explain` adds "this is the class of fixed defect N (`<entry id>`): the defect is back, or this is a new symptom in the same class."
5. Every directory under `proof/controls/` is named by an entry of one of the two registers.
6. A directory a fixed entry names is never retained again (`proof/checks/controls.py::retain` removes the directory before it writes). A remaining defect whose symptom moves on such a document gets a new control under a new directory name.
7. `values` on a fixed entry needs no targeted document: it has none.
8. The repair rule (below).

There is deliberately no rule "a fixed entry matches nothing on any corpus document". Classes are coarse (`case_blocks@vellum@*` `/runs/*/cases/*`, or a trace's case name text on `trace@local.ccz`), so a later, different defect registered live in the same class would fail the lane on a difference the register holds. | Design | Why not |
|---|---|
| A `fixed` marker on entries kept in `known-defects.json` | `reconcile` would have to skip marked entries on corpus documents or hold a regression with the entry meant to catch it, and the exit's clause that the register holds no entry of a step 2 defect could not be read from the file. |
| An expectations file inside each control directory | It would be an input file of the control (`proof/observe/unit.py::input_files` reads the directory), so editing a judge input would re-key the observation. |
| Drop a fixed defect's control and rely on each check's planted-failure tests | It abandons step 1's decision 12: the retained pre-fix input is the only thing that catches a fix pull request that also changes a comparator, a rule or `proof/checks/compare/names.py` so that the check goes blind. |

**The repair rule.** A fixed entry is repaired in exactly two ways, each named in its pull request with the citation:

| What happened | Repair |
|---|---|
| The check's vocabulary or the pinned upstream text moved (a renamed Vellum message, a moved HQ function, a renamed value class) and the control shows the same symptom at a new path | Re-path the entry from the control's evidence. |
| Upstream changed the behavior so the pre-fix bytes are no longer a symptom, or a judge change deliberately stops reporting the class on the control too, or a change to how the lane runs a control (the privileges its oracle grants it) takes the symptom off the control with no configuration left that shows it | Delete the entry, and the control directory when no other entry names it. |

A fixed entry is never loosened, never widened to a pattern, and its control is never retained again. A fixed entry that stops showing for any other reason is the regression in the harness the control exists to catch. The weekly pin pull request's prewarm, and the lane with every reader beside it, observe every control fresh (`proof/README.md`, "Changing a pin"), so both repairs surface there and are committed to that branch before it merges.

### The held walk stops

**Today.** The lane holds a second strict list beside the register, `proof/walk-stops.json`: each place a reader's walk of a document ends short of the app's own end (`proof/checks/stops.py`; `proof/README.md`, "Walk stops"). Proofs 3 and 4 compare two states' walks, so a run cut short alike on both sides passes with the rest of it unseen; the check `stops` (`proof/checks/test_stops.py`, one item for each document and each control an entry of the register names) reads each walk on its own, and the gate holds what it showed in a section of its own, "Walk stops".

- An entry is `{"document", "reader", "cause", "reason"}`. `reader` is `formplayer`, `webapps` or `preview` (the Web Apps client in App Preview). `cause` is a class written from the reader's own words (`notification:unable-to-find-lookup-table`, `case-not-listed`), never from a name the app authored. `reason` is one line a reviewer can check at the reader's source or in the record.
- The list is strict both ways, as the register is: a stop no entry holds fails its document's check and the gate by name, and so does an entry whose stop no run of its document showed.
- An entry is the reader's own end, never an answer the lane failed to give: where a worker would get past the screen, the walk gives what a worker gives.
- An entry holds the walks of its document and of the control of the same name, and it must show on the corpus document. The loader refuses an entry that names a document the corpus does not hold.
- Every reader a stop names is a served one, so a run without the readers holds none of the list and leaves it to the run with every reader.

At the lane's head the list holds 33 entries on 28 documents. Three parts of that contract meet step 2:

- **A document that leaves the corpus.** Executed during planning, without the readers, on the exit-state stack: with `targeted-supply-point-read` gone and its entry kept, `proof/checks/test_stops.py::test_the_committed_list_loads_and_names_documents_the_corpus_holds` fails.
- **A stop a fix removes.** Its entry then fails as unshown, and the message says to remove it.
- **A control keeps stopping.** A control retains the bytes from before its fix, so its walks end where the document's did. While the document's entry stands it holds them. Once that entry is removed, or where a control carries another name than its document, `proof/checks/stops.py::unheld` reports the control's stop as one no entry holds, and the control's check and the gate fail. This is the register's own problem of a control whose document went quiet, which the fixed-defect register answers for a difference, and nothing in the lane answers it for a stop. Executed during planning (the stops slice, under "Fix"): on the exit state, with each moved entry removed the gate fails on all eight controls by name, and with the entries left on their documents it fails on the six documents the corpus still holds and on two controls.

**Fix.** A held stop moves with its fix, as a register entry does. An entry names where its stop shows by exactly one of two keys: `document`, as today, or `control`, the name of a directory under `proof/controls/`.

```json
{"control": "targeted-close-condition-unparsable", "reader": "formplayer",
 "cause": "notification:encountered-a-problem-with-display-condition",
 "reason": "The control retains the form from before step 2 moved the close condition: Core does not parse it, so Formplayer cannot install the app."}
```

A `control` entry holds that control's walks and no document's. It is strict both ways on the control: a stop of the control that no entry holds fails, and so does a `control` entry no run of its control showed. A `document` entry is as today, and keeps holding the control of the same name while it stands.

Four things make that hold through a run and a gate:

- **Every control the list names is read.** The stops check and the queue read the controls the held entries of both registers name and the controls the list's `control` entries name, for the readers the run observes. Without that, a control only the list names, or one named only by register entries of a reader the run leaves out, is read by no item, and its entry fails as one no shard ran. `targeted-invalid-question-ids-after-9` is that case: its three register entries are Android's, so a run without a device queues it only through the list.
- **The gate carries a `control` entry through a selection.** Where a run names its documents by hand and did not queue an entry's control, the gate leaves that `control` entry to the nightly and says how many it left, as it leaves a `document` entry whose document the selection did not run. A sampled run and a whole run hold every `control` entry.
- **One stop, one entry.** The loader refuses a `control` entry beside the `document` entry of the same name, reader and cause: the document's entry already holds that control, so the pair would hold one stop twice. A stop moves by one entry changing its key, never by a second entry.
- **What must show where.** A `document` entry must show on its document and need not show on the control of its name, which is what lets a control retained for another reason run under its document's entry. A `control` entry must show on its control, which is what makes a moved entry strict.

Executed during planning, with the design written as scratch on the exit-state stack and the stops check run with the Web Apps reader, which brings Formplayer, over the eight controls and the six of their documents the corpus still holds: each control shows its stop and passes on its `control` entry; each document shows no stop and passes with no entry; `targeted-invalid-question-ids-after-9`, which no register entry named in that run, ran and passed through its `control` entry; and the gate's walk-stops section holds. With the moved entries removed, which is the lane's contract of today once each fix takes the stop off its document, that section fails on all eight controls by name. With the entries left on their documents it fails on the six documents, each as an entry no run of its document shows, and on the two controls no document's entry can hold: `targeted-invalid-question-ids-after-9`, which carries another name than its document, and `targeted-supply-point-read`, whose document left the corpus. So no list in the lane's form of today holds the exit state, and this one does.

The rule for every pull request of the stack:

1. A pull request that removes a document from the corpus, or whose fix removes the cause of a held stop, changes that stop's entry in the same change. Where a control keeps the bytes from before the fix and its walks still stop there, the entry's `document` key becomes `control`, naming that control, with reader and cause unchanged and a reason that says what the control retains. Where no control shows the stop, the entry is removed.
2. A pull request that retains a control under another name than its document's, from a document that holds a stop, adds a `control` entry for that control where its walks show the stop.
3. A stop a pull request's reader slice shows that no entry holds is the walk's to fix where a worker would get past it. Where the reader itself ends there on every state alike, the pull request adds the entry with its reason, which is the lane's own rule.
4. No entry is added for a stop that shows on one state and not on another: that is a difference, and proofs 3 and 4 report it.

## Why `proof/identity-moves.json` stays empty

`proof/identity-moves.json` gains no entry in step 2, and the outline's "adds the identity moves it decides" is withdrawn.

- Proof 1 compares A with B, A with B-edit outside the edit's footprint, and `local.ccz` with `local-again.ccz` (`proof/checks/proof1.py::document_identity`). All three compare two exports of the current document by the current code. A stored-document migration, an emitter rename, a moved case block or a renamed language code changes both sides alike and shows in none of them. On a control both sides are pre-fix bytes.
- A move accepts its path on every document (`proof/checks/registers.py::accepted_moves`), so a move for, say, `/lookup_tables/*/id` would hide the very regression a fix removes.
- A lane judgment across the step (a control's A against the same document's A today) was considered and rejected: it needs a second `a` record per control on every cold run, it could say nothing about ids (which the ledger preserves, proven in Nova against real Postgres), and the lane holds no deployment made before the step.

The step's one-time identity changes are real, and they exist only in deployments and installs made before step 2. They are held in three places: each fix's "Identity" part in this plan; the cutover's notice, which names every app, entity and deployment a change touches; and the cutover's pure tests over the frozen pre-step fixtures (`scripts/lib/hqRoundTripCutover/__tests__/fixtures/pre-step/`), which assert each moved wire identity by parsing the pre-step emission and the post-step one, never by pattern. Those fixtures leave with the tooling in pull request 16, after the cutover has run; the notices are the durable record.

## Corpus changes

`proof/corpus/emitCorpus.ts::admittedDoc` stops the emission on a document the strict schema or full validation refuses, so every narrowing rewrites or removes the documents it refuses in the same pull request.

**The rule.** Each pull request that narrows the validator or changes the model runs the emission first, with no Docker, at its base and at its head:

```bash
node --conditions=react-server --import ./proof/corpus/entropy.mts \
  --import tsx proof/corpus/emit.ts --out <dir>
```

and reads three things: the stop, if any (the first refused document, by name); the census of documents the export boundary left out; and the diff of `index.json`'s document ids. An expander document's id is `expander-<slug of its first test's title>-<hash of that title>-<index among that test's documents>` (`proof/corpus/documents.ts::readExpanderCapture`), so retitling a test in `lib/commcare/__tests__/expander.test.ts`, or changing how many documents it expands, renames documents that entries, `proof/rules/conftest.py::DOCUMENTS` and `proof/timings.json` name. An id that changes is either restored (the test keeps its title and count) or followed through every one of those. A refused fuzz document means the generator is changed to stop drawing the shape (`proof/corpus/fuzzSample.ts::admitted` throws); no entry names a fuzz document. A generator maps the draw after it is made and removes none, so the random stream and every other draw stay. An entity a fixture or an expander test gains carries a named uuid: `lib/__tests__/docHelpers.ts::buildDoc` mints an unnamed entity's uuid from one counter, so an unnamed addition moves the identities of every document built after it (executed during planning: the three entities pull request 10 adds, unnamed, changed the stored bytes of 201 documents; named, of 30; part 08, What every block below shares).

## The lane's cost, and the runs that prove each kind of fix
