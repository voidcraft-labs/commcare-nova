---
title: Manage access
description: What permissions your client gets and how to revoke them.
---

When your client signs in to Nova, you see a consent screen listing what it's asking for. Each row is one permission your client can use. You can revoke the connection from your client or from Nova at any time.

## What your client can do

| Permission | What it grants |
|---|---|
| See your name and email | Standard sign-in identity. |
| Read your apps | List, search, and summarize apps in your Nova account. |
| Create and edit apps on your behalf | Build new apps and modify existing ones. |
| See your CommCare HQ project connection | Look up which HQ project spaces your stored API key can reach. Used to preview the target before an upload. |
| Deploy your apps to CommCare HQ on your behalf | Upload finished apps to a chosen HQ project space. |
| Read apps and data in your CommCare HQ project spaces, see who in Nova manages them, and bring them into your Projects on your behalf | List a project space's apps and read one, with its lookup tables and places, using your stored API key, see who in Nova already manages an app there, and bring it into a Nova Project, where every member of that Project can see it. This permission also lets a client bring in later changes, refresh HQ-managed tables and places during an upload, and refresh the settings HQ keeps for an app. |
| Download private Project data assignments and worker installation data for project spaces you can access | Prepare a private CommCare HQ import file that carries which lookup rows belong to which workers, groups and places, and the place ID workbook a publish asks for, for a project space your account publishes to. Your account still needs edit access to the Project and a current connection to that project space. |
| See the members of your Projects — names and emails | List who's in your shared Projects and which invitations are pending. (Listing the Projects themselves rides the base connection; this row is about the member roster.) |
| Create Projects, invite teammates, and change member roles on your behalf | Create shared Projects, send invitations, change member roles, and move apps between Projects — acts that give other people access to your work. |

The HQ rows are separate from the rest because CommCare HQ is a different system: having Nova access doesn't automatically grant HQ access. The Projects rows are separate because they reach beyond your own account — one exposes other members' names and emails, the other hands out access. Each of these permissions stands alone (the write side doesn't include the read side, and neither includes bringing apps in), so check every row your client needs. A connection made before bringing apps in had its own row, and that could already see your CommCare HQ connection and deploy to it, can bring apps in too: it picks that up the next time your client renews its sign-in, with nothing to approve. Any other connection, a new one included, is asked when a tool needs a permission it doesn't hold, the same as any permission you haven't granted. If your client can't ask, the affected tools refuse with a clear error and you can reauthorize.

## Disconnecting

The cleanest way is from your client's MCP settings, most clients have a "sign out" or "remove server" action that tells Nova to invalidate the connection.

If your client doesn't, or if you've already removed Nova from your client's config, do it from Nova: open [Settings → Connected apps](https://commcare.app/settings), find the client's entry, and remove it. Once the connection is gone, the next tool call from that client fails until you sign back in.

If your client uses an [API key](./api-keys.mdx) instead of OAuth, it doesn't appear under Connected apps, find it under **API keys** on the same settings page and click **Revoke**. The two paths are independent: revoking an OAuth grant doesn't touch your API keys, and revoking a key doesn't disturb any active OAuth sessions.

## Reauthorizing

When an OAuth tool call needs an HQ or Projects permission you have not granted, Nova sends your client a permission challenge before running the tool. Clients that support incremental consent can open the authorization screen for you, keeping your existing permissions.

If your client does not open that screen, you can sign out and back in. The new sign-in shows the consent screen again, make sure every row you need is checked before you click Allow.

When Nova adds a new permission, how a client picks it up depends on how the client identifies itself. An app that identifies itself by a web address (the consent screen shows it as **Identified by**) picks up newly added permissions on its own, the next time Nova re-reads the app's details. Signing out and back in is all you need.

Nova added the permission to bring apps in from CommCare HQ to existing client registrations, including older registrations. If your connection already had both HQ permissions when this feature arrived, it picks up the third when it renews. Otherwise your client can ask you to grant it.

For other new permissions, an app registered the older way may still offer only the permissions it requested when it registered. If it receives `invalid_scope`, you can remove the Nova server from the client and add it again to register with the current set.

## Temporary service interruptions

If Nova says it could not finish checking a request, your client can retry the existing connection when the service recovers. This response means Nova could not check access; it does not mean your credentials were rejected. A temporary startup failure also recovers on a later request.
