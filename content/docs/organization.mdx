---
title: Organization
description: Describe where people work, and control what each place owns and who can see it.
---

Apps that serve more than one site need to know about places. A referral has to
land somewhere; a supervisor should see their whole district while a clinic
worker sees only their clinic; a form needs to name the site a worker belongs
to. **App setup → Organization** is where you describe that structure.

There are three things on that screen, and the order is the order you build
them: the **levels** of your organization, the **information** its places
carry, and the **places** themselves.

## Levels

A level is a rung — Region, District, Facility, Ward. You add them top down,
each sitting under the one above it.

Each level answers two questions, and the screen keeps them apart on purpose,
because they are genuinely separate and mixing them up is the most common way an
app ends up behaving strangely.

### Cases: what a worker receives

**People work here** decides whether workers can be assigned to places at this
level.

**Places here own cases** decides whether cases can belong to a place at this
level.

Those two are independent, and all four combinations are useful:

- **Both on** — an ordinary place people work out of. Workers assigned there
  receive the cases their place owns.
- **Owns cases, nobody works here** — a queue. Cases live there and reach
  nobody's device by assignment, so they stay out of every worker's app while
  remaining searchable and addressable. This is how you keep a large backlog
  from being downloaded onto every phone.
- **People work here, owns nothing** — an oversight rung. Everything those
  workers receive comes from the setting below.
- **Neither** — pure structure, a rung that exists so places below it have
  something to sit in.

**Workers also receive cases from places below** widens what an assigned worker
gets. "Everything below" is the common answer. Stopping at a particular level
needs a setting enabled on the CommCare project you deploy to — Nova tells you
that where you choose it, and Deployment lists it as a prerequisite, because
without it workers would quietly receive everything below instead.

### What workers here can see

Seeing a place and receiving its cases are different things, and this is the
setting people most often expect to do both. It does not. Widening it lets your
expressions **name** more places; it moves no cases at all.

The choices:

- **Their own place, everything under it, and the chain above** — the default,
  and right for most levels.
- **Their own place, but only certain levels** — narrows a large tree to the
  rungs that matter.
- **Everything under a level further up** — lets a worker address places beside
  their own, such as the other clinics in their district.
- **The whole organization** — every place, for apps small enough to afford it.

The address book ships to every device, so widening it costs download size for
every worker at that level. That is the trade you are making.

## Place information

Places already carry a name and a code. **Place information** is anything else
you need on them — a phone number, a catchment population, an opening date.

Each entry has a name people read and a key CommCare stores it under. Nova
checks that key as you type against the rules CommCare applies, and this matters
more than it looks: CommCare accepts a key it does not recognize without
complaining and stores it where nothing will ever read it. A typo would not fail
loudly; it would quietly do nothing. So Nova refuses the bad key up front
instead.

An entry applies to every level by default. Narrow it when only some kinds of
place have that information.

## Places

The places themselves — your actual districts and facilities. Unlike levels,
these are data: you can add thousands, and later push them to CommCare.

Each place sits at a level and inside a place above it. **A place may skip a
level** — if some regions run districts and some do not, a facility can hang
directly off its region. Nova does not make you invent a placeholder district,
because a made-up place is worse than an absent one: expressions that look for
the district would find the fake one and give a confident wrong answer, where a
genuine gap gives an honest empty one.

One thing to know if you plan to publish: CommCare's own interface cannot create
a place that skips a level. Your app previews and exports correctly either way,
and Deployment tells you before it tries.

Every place gets a **code** when you create it, and that code never changes —
renaming a place leaves it alone. The code is what bulk uploads and CommCare use
to identify the place, so it has to stay put even as the name changes.

### Archiving

Places are archived rather than deleted, because deleting one would strand the
cases it owns with no way back.

Archiving a place also archives everything under it, and Nova tells you exactly
what will happen before you confirm:

- how many places go with it;
- which personas stop working there — their next remaining place becomes their
  main one, and a persona left with nowhere carries no location information at
  all;
- how many cases are owned there. **Those cases do not move.** They stay where
  they are and stop reaching anyone, which is what CommCare itself does. Bringing
  the place back is what makes them reachable again.

Bringing a place back also restores the path to it, since a place is unreachable
while anything above it is archived. It does not restore assignments — those are
your edits now, and Nova will not overwrite a deliberate change with the memory
of an older one.

## Assigning a persona

A persona's places are set on **App setup → Users & personas**, under **Where
they work**. The first place in the list is their main one; use **Make main** to
promote another.

A persona assigned nowhere carries no location information at all — the same as
an unassigned CommCare worker. That is deliberate rather than an oversight: it
is exactly what the app would see, so a condition that depends on a worker's
location behaves in preview the way it will on a device.
