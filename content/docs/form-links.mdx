---
title: Send people somewhere after a form is submitted
description: Choose where the app goes next, with links that depend on the case and the person.
---

When a form is submitted, the app has to go somewhere. Often the right place
depends on what was just saved: a screening that found a risk should open the
referral form, a visit that closed the case should go back to the list, and
everything else should return to the module.

**After submit** is where a form says that. It holds a short list of
destinations, each a form or a module to open and, if you like, the condition
it runs under, and an **Otherwise** row that says where the form goes when no
destination with a condition or a carried case runs.

## Where to find it

Open a form, choose the settings gear, and find **After submit**. With no
destinations yet, the row says where the form goes and offers the built-in
destinations, **Another form or module**, and **Add destinations**. Once a
destination exists it says what runs, such as **Runs 2 destinations in
order**, with the form's Otherwise beside it, and offers **Edit destinations**.

The After submit screen has its own address, so you can link a teammate
straight to it. The **Preview** control runs the form the links belong to, and
Preview runs the destinations the same way the app does.

## The order matters

The list is headed **After this form, these run in order**. Every destination
whose condition is true runs, from the top down: on Android the next one opens
when the worker finishes the one before it, and in Web Apps only the first one
that applies opens. Each row says when it runs: **Every time**, **When** its
condition is true, and, for a destination that carries a case, **When the case
it carries is on the worker's device**. Two destinations can both apply at the
same time, so order is part of what you are saying.

Add one with **Go somewhere every time** or **Go somewhere when a condition is
true**. Drag a destination by its handle, or focus the handle and use the arrow
keys (**Home** and **End** jump to the ends). Any destination can go in any
position.

## Otherwise

The **Otherwise** row is always last. It is where the form goes when no
destination with a condition or a carried case runs, and it shows once a
destination has one. It opens before a destination that runs every time.
**Otherwise, go somewhere else** sets it, and until a destination has a
condition the builder says **Give a destination a condition first, so there is
a time when none runs.**

With no Otherwise, the app goes home when no destination runs, and the row
reads **Otherwise, back to the app home**. The same choices say where a form
with no destinations goes:

- **App home**: back to the main screen. On Android that is the app's home
  screen; in Web Apps it is the app's first screen, which lists its modules.
  For the Otherwise it is **The app home**, which leaves the form without one.
- **First menu**: the app's first menu, which lists its modules. In Web Apps
  this is the same screen as App home; on Android, App home opens the home
  screen and First menu opens that list.
- **This module**: open the module at its normal starting screen.
- **Parent module**: for a form in a submenu, open the module above it, with
  the case it selected still selected.
- **Previous screen**: return to the preceding selection or task. When the
  form was chosen before its record, its record picker opens again for that
  same form. When the record was chosen before the form, the module menu opens
  with that record still selected. This follows the app's task sequence.
- **Another form or module**: open a form, or a module's form list. For a
  form with no destinations it opens the destinations screen, where you add
  one; for the Otherwise it is a link of its own.

When a form gains its first destination with a condition, where it went after
submit becomes its Otherwise, and Nova says so. When every destination runs
each time again, the Otherwise has nothing left to cover, so Nova removes it
and tells you.

### Which destinations a form is offered

The list only offers destinations the form's module can return to, so a
choice you see is one CommCare can build:

- **Previous screen** isn't offered in a module that selects several cases,
  under a module that does, or in a menu whose forms read the search. The usual
  destination there is **This module**, or **First menu** under a module that
  selects several cases.
- **This module** isn't offered under a module that selects several cases.
- **Parent module** is offered only to a form in a submenu, and not while the
  module above it selects several cases.
- A form in a module that is off the menu isn't offered its own module or the
  previous screen, and a form isn't offered the module above it while that
  module is off the menu. Its forms go to **First menu** instead. See
  [Display conditions](/docs/display-conditions) for what "off the menu" means.

**Module** isn't offered in a menu that shows its forms in the menu above, and
**Parent menu** isn't offered under one, because neither has a screen of its
own. **Previous screen** isn't offered to a form shown on the app's first menu.

When a change to a module takes a destination away from one of its forms,
Nova moves the form to the nearest one still offered and tells you.

## What a condition can read

A link's condition runs after the form has closed. That decides what it can use:

- **The case the form just saved**, including the values this very submission
  wrote to it. A link on a visit form can read the status the visit just set,
  and a link on a registration form can read the case it just created.
- **The person using the app**: their worker information.
- **Project data and places**: a row of a data table, or a place in your
  organization.
- **What a search found**, when the form opened in a menu whose forms read
  the search, such as how many cases it returned.

It cannot read the form's answers, because by the time the link runs the form
is gone. If a link should depend on something someone typed, save that answer
to a case property and read the property instead. The condition editor only
offers what a link can use, and says so when you reach for a form answer.

## Carry values

Some destinations open straight away. Others open a case, and need to know
which one. The link's detail screen shows what its destination needs:

- When this form opened or created a case of the right type, the link carries
  it automatically: **Carry it automatically** says which case travels with the
  person.
- When it cannot, choose **Work it out here** and give the case id the
  destination should open. The screen lists every value the destination needs;
  a link carries all of them or none of them, so there is no half-filled
  handover.
- A destination whose forms read the search needs to fetch the carried case
  before it opens. **Carry it automatically** supports the case this form
  selected or created. An explicit assignment to that same case works too. A different
  manual assignment may be unavailable; Nova names the link and offers
  automatic matching or a destination whose forms read their own case, which
  supports manual values.
- A destination that carries a case runs only when the worker's device holds
  that case. A value that names no case of that type never opens the
  destination; the next one, or the form's Otherwise, runs instead.
- A module's form list needs nothing carried: the person picks a case when
  they arrive. A link to a module carries no values.

## Start this form again

**Start this form again** is the first choice in the destination picker. It
opens a fresh copy of the same form, which suits a form workers fill in many
times in a row. It keeps working if the form moves to another module. A
destination can also open a form whose own destinations lead back here.

## What Nova refuses

Nova keeps the list in a shape the app can run, and says why before anything
changes:

- A link to a submenu CommCare HQ can't link to from this form's menu: choose
  the menu above it or a form inside it. A form can link to a submenu only
  from a submenu whose parent has the same case type, and the target picker
  says so beside each one it can't offer.
- A link whose destination needs a case this form cannot hand over. Carry the
  value by hand, or choose a destination this form can pass its case to.
- An Otherwise that could never run, because every destination runs each time.
  Give a destination a condition first.
- A link to a module that is off the menu. It would open a menu with nothing
  in it on Android and the app's first screen in Web Apps, so point the link at
  a form inside the module, or show the module on the menu again.

## When the destination isn't on the menu

A link can point at a form someone can't see on their menu: a form that is off
the menu, a form inside a module that is off the menu, or a form or module
whose display condition is false for the person submitting. Android and Web
Apps handle these differently:

- **A form that is off the menu, or hidden by its display condition**: Android
  opens it. Web Apps doesn't, and lands on the module that holds it, listing
  its other forms.
- **A form inside a module that is off the menu**: Android opens the form. Web
  Apps lands on the app's first screen.
- **A module hidden by its display condition**: Android opens it with nothing
  in it, and Web Apps opens the app's first screen instead.

The link's detail screen says this under a link whose destination some workers
may not see. Preview does what the platform it runs as does, as the list above
describes for each case.

## On a device

On Android every destination that applies runs in order; in Web Apps the
first does. The Otherwise runs when no destination with a condition or a
carried case runs, and a carried case opens the destination directly. What you
see in Preview is what the app does on the platform Preview runs as.
